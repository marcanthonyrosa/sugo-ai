# Writing as Sugo AI

Full rules: `docs/brand/MESSAGING_AND_VOICE.md`. Facts and boilerplate:
`docs/brand/04-BRAND_FACTS.md`.

## Message hierarchy

Use the highest-level message that fits the space; add detail only when the
reader needs it.

**Positioning** — Sugo AI is a product studio for traditional businesses building
serious software.

**Headline** — Modern product development for companies that aren't software
companies.

**Explanation** — We help traditional businesses ship internal tools, AI agents,
and customer products with the discipline of a modern software company.

**Differentiation** — We are not a strategy shop that disappears after the deck.
We are not a dev shop waiting for tickets. We are a product studio that stays
close to the work and remains accountable for what ships.

These are anchors, not boilerplate. Adapt to the channel; preserve the audience,
the operating standard, and the ownership promise.

## The story, in five moves

1. Traditional businesses have deep domain expertise and real constraints.
2. What's missing is a product-development system that turns that into adopted,
   production-ready software.
3. Sugo brings the discipline of a modern software company across product,
   design, and engineering.
4. Sugo works close to operators and owns the path from first decision through
   production and rollout.
5. The result is useful software and a better next decision — not a deck, a
   disconnected prototype, or a pile of tickets.

Not every piece needs all five. A post might carry one.

## Voice

**Plain, not simplified.** Write for smart operators who don't live in software
jargon. "We map the handoffs, approvals, edge cases, and dependencies" — not
"cross-functional alignment across the value stream."

**Specific, not inflated.** "Code in the production system" — not "best-in-class
end-to-end digital transformation." Let specificity create authority.

**Confident, not absolute.** "We'll tell you what looks contained, what looks
larger, and where the uncertainty is" — not "we guarantee rapid results."

**Warm, not performative.** A thoughtful senior partner talking to another adult.
No corporate distance, no forced friendliness, no sales pressure.

**Product-literate, not technical for its own sake.** Connect design and
engineering detail to adoption, trust, reliability, or the work people do.
Technical depth is useful when it changes the decision.

**Quietly playful.** One dry beat per section, maximum. Never stack jokes. Never
joke about operational pain, failed projects, money, or buyer uncertainty.

## Mechanics

- Sentence case headings
- Short declarative sentences with a few longer explanatory ones
- Active voice, concrete verbs: build, ship, map, decide, test, learn, adopt
- Display headlines under ~50 characters; 70 is the ceiling
- Buttons short and action-led: "Start a conversation," "See how we work"
- Curly quotes, real apostrophes, em dashes
- No exclamation marks. No emoji.
- "We" for the practice; "I" when Marc speaks personally
- **Product studio**, **practice**, **product partner** — never agency,
  consultancy, firm, or shop

## Audience language

Primary phrase: **traditional businesses**. Plain-English shorthand: **companies
that aren't software companies.**

Describe them by their strengths: strong operators, deep domain knowledge, real
constraints, regulated or operationally complex environments, increasing
dependence on software.

**Never** call them digitally immature, old-fashioned, behind, resistant, or
unsophisticated. Never imply Sugo knows their business better than they do.

## Preferred language

serious software · modern product development · the discipline of a modern
software company · product judgment · internal tools and AI agents · customer
products · real constraints · close to the work · through production · production
readiness · real rollout · version one · working software · what actually ships

Use **AI-native** carefully. It can describe an operating standard; it should not
replace "modern software company" as the main promise unless AI is central to the
piece.

## Never

seamless · unleash · empower · supercharge · transform · journey · innovative
solutions · next-generation · in today's digital landscape · clarity, structure,
and craft used without concrete meaning

Also avoid vague phrases about messy workflows, stakeholder alignment, or
innovation unless the copy names what is actually happening.

## Claims

- No invented metrics, timelines, testimonials, client names, outcomes, or dates.
- No delivery timeline before the workflow, constraints, and meaning of "shipped"
  are understood.
- Price is a function of problem shape, systems involved, and how much of the path
  to production already exists.
- **One** citable figure: MIT NANDA, *The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business*,
  August 2025 — approximately 5% of enterprise GenAI pilots showed measurable P&L
  impact. Written `≈5%`, never "95% of AI projects fail." The citation travels
  with it every time.
- Reconfirm founder and client outcome claims before a new external channel.

## Plain-language definitions

**AI agent** — software that carries out a multi-step task using defined rules,
tools, and data. Still needs ownership, boundaries, and a way to handle
exceptions.

**Internal tool** — software for people inside the business. "Internal" doesn't
make the workflow simpler; it often just makes the complexity less visible.

**Pilot** — a trial run. It matters only when someone owns what happens after the
demo.

**Production** — when software stops being a demonstration and starts being how
the work gets done.

**Rollout** — making software part of the organization that must use, support, and
trust it. Launch is an event; rollout is adoption.

## By channel

**Website** — lead with audience and offer; lifecycle ownership builds
credibility; keep the scan path obvious.

**LinkedIn** — lead with one concrete observation from the work. Teach before
selling. Short Sugo connection at the end, not the full positioning.

**Proposals** — start with the buyer's workflow and constraints. Use the Sugo
framework to explain ownership, not to sound pre-packaged.

**Articles** — write for operators and leaders making software decisions inside
real businesses. Thinking from the work, not commentary from a distance.

## Before publishing

- Is it clear who this is for?
- Does it respect the audience's domain expertise?
- Does it name real work, decisions, or consequences?
- Could a generic consultancy make the same claim? If yes, rewrite.
- Is every proof point supportable and current?
- Is the tone calm, direct, warm, hype-free?
- Is the playful beat helping, or decorating?
- Is `marc@sugoai.com` used for direct contact?
