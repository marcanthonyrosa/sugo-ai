Illustration lab — beyond the pot.

Dodonut’s bird works because it isn’t a drawing — it’s an assembly of flat primitives in panel colors: a circle, a quarter-circle beak, a half-round crest. This lab applies that grammar to Sugo’s kitchen, then tests marks, character, and placement — on the Viola Light system, set in Familjen Grotesk.

Direction 1 · Geo-tomato

The bird-language, applied to the tomato.

Circle body, two quarter-disc leaves at different sizes, a wedge stem, one glint. No outlines — the shapes carry it.

Direction 2 · Geo-pot

The pot as flat assembly.

Round-bottomed vessel, lid bar with a dot knob, half-round handles, steam as ascending dots. Shape does the identifying — no color dependence, no outline.

Direction 3 · The Simmer mark

A logo candidate: shallow pan, three curls of steam.

Filled silhouette plus round-capped strokes and a lot of negative space. Built to survive from footer sign-off down to favicon.

The mark simplifies honestly as it shrinks: three curls → one curl → pan-plus-curl. That stepped reduction is a system, not a compromise — same move Dodonut makes when the bird becomes a beak glyph.

Direction 4 · Character pot

The current linework pot — with more life, no face.

Same 4px navy language the hero already speaks. Added: an S-curve of steam, two bubbles breaking the sauce line, a lid leaning against the pot, one falling basil leaf.

Direction 5 · The peek

Placement personality — the Dodonut-bird move.

Not a different mark: the same objects, placed with intent. The bird’s charm is that it interrupts the layout politely.

Notes

Read before choosing.

  • The Simmer mark scales best. It has a designed reduction path (three curls → one curl → pan-glyph) and it pairs naturally with squared-radius carpentry — strokes and slab silhouette, no soft blobs. Favicon, wordmark neighbor, and footer sign-off in one asset.
  • The geo-tomato needs red to be a tomato. In navy or saffron it’s a lovely fruit-form, not a tomato — the circle is too pure to identify itself. Use it only if the coral colorway (mark-scope, ≤2–3% viewport) is acceptable, or use it deliberately cropped (the peek), where ambiguity becomes charm.
  • The geo-pot is the honest bird-equivalent. Its silhouette carries meaning with zero color dependence, it holds the hero, and it miniaturizes to section accents. If you want one geometric assembly in the system, it’s this one.
  • The character pot answers “boring” without a rebrand. Bubbles, the leaning lid, and the falling basil add narrative (someone is cooking) inside the exact linework the hero already uses. Lowest risk, highest continuity.
  • Memphis-drift warning. Flat primitives go generic fast when angles and radii are arbitrary. This lab keeps leaf rotations at ±14–18° and reuses two radii families; whichever direction wins should lock those numbers as tokens, the way colors already are.
  • Recommendation: character pot in the hero · Simmer mark as logo/favicon/footer sign-off · peek placements for footer personality · geo-pot as the section-accent miniature. The geo-tomato retires to the peek slot, where it’s at its best.
