The mark says two things at once: sauce, and software heritage — the pixel grid carries the 80s/90s digital memory the studio’s discipline descends from. The open question isn’t the tomato; it’s how much red, and where it’s allowed to live.

Sprite I — “Chunky.”

A rebuild of your first reference: ~14-cell grid, flat body, plus-shaped stem with detached nubs, diagonal glint. No outline — the silhouette does the work, which is what lets it survive tiny sizes.

Sprite II — “Sixteen-bit.”

A rebuild of your second reference: ~20-cell grid, pixel outline, maroon shade run lower-right, pale glint, multi-sprig stem. More nostalgia, more rendering — a sprite from a game, not a glyph from a system.

The pixel pot.

The hybrid: the Panel Press sauce pot rebuilt on the same grid language. One bridge between the hero art you have and the pixel direction you’re testing.

Navy vessel, gold band, one coral sauce line, steam that steps up the grid a cell at a time. The sauce line is the only red on the object — the mark-scope license applied to the pot.

If the pixel direction wins, this replaces the smooth-line pot in the hero and the tomato becomes the favicon and small-scale mark — one grid language across every size.

What we’d recommend testing.

  • Chunky is the system sprite. It survives 16 px, its blunt geometry matches the 2 px border-carpentry, and the plus-stem is distinctive enough to own.
  • Pomodoro coral solves the red worry from inside the live guide — it’s already your garnish color. Full tomato red stays available under a mark-scope license: the mark only, a few percent of a viewport, never a surface.
  • Duotone navy is the zero-red fallback for dark zones, report covers, and red-sensitive clients — and it doubles as a natural footer/ghost state of the same asset.
  • Gold harvest is a maybe. Without the outline it drifts toward pumpkin; with the sixteen-bit outline it holds. Only worth pursuing if the palette lands gold-forward (judge it against 1B).
  • Sixteen-bit is the poster asset, not the mark. More nostalgia, more charm at large sizes, but it muddies small and introduces a second outline language. Keep it for moments — a 404, a writing header — while the chunky sprite runs the system. The pixel grid can quietly extend (pot steam, a divider, the favicon) but should stay accent-scale, never theme-park.
